**Awareness**
<div style="margin-top: 1.0rem;"></div>

What is awareness and why do we want it
<div style="margin-top: 1.0rem;"></div>

Awareness means mindfulness and responsibility in social interactions. It is about creating an environment in which all people feel safe, respected and welcome, regardless of gender, origin, sexual orientation, disability, body, age or experience on the bike.
<div style="margin-top: 1.0rem;"></div>

Awareness structures are not control, they are support systems and clear attitudes:
- *Awareness persons* are contact people during events or group rides. They listen, take those affected seriously and support them when boundaries have been crossed, whether verbally, physically or emotionally.
<div style="margin-top: 1.0rem;"></div>

- *Awareness also means prevention*: We raise awareness of discriminatory or inappropriate behaviour and promote interaction based on consent, consideration and self-reflection.
<div style="margin-top: 1.0rem;"></div>

- *Awareness requires clear concepts*: It must be defined who in the community can be contacted, how situations are addressed and which steps follow in the case of an assault, a boundary violation or a conflict. 
<div style="margin-top: 1.0rem;"></div>

This protects all involved, meaning those affected, awareness persons and the community itself, because it provides orientation, creates reliability and ensures confidence in taking action. 
<div style="margin-top: 1.0rem;"></div>

We need awareness structures because equality, inclusion and mutual respect do not happen on their own. Awareness creates trust, strengthens the community and ensures that our sport and scene are accessible to everyone, not only to those who already feel safe. 
What our awareness concept will look like in detail is something we want to develop together in the WA group safe.space.lab💜
